💡 Tips & Logistics
The Hike
Proper shoes required — not sandals. Pack 2L water each, sunscreen, hats. Well-marked trail with a mix of paved and rocky terrain.
Tender Tips
Get early tender tickets immediately on embarkation morning. The cable car from the old port gets extremely crowded — take it up, walk the 587 stairs down for the views.
August Heat Warning
30–35°C on an exposed volcanic ridge. Starting at 7 AM is essential. Bring electrolytes — not just water. The views are worth every step.
Getting Around
KTEL buses are cheap and reliable. Taxis are expensive — share at taxi stands. ATV rentals popular but risky on narrow roads. Oia is very walkable once you're there.
👕 What to Wear
🥾
Real hiking shoes or trainers — not sandals. The Fira–Oia caldera trail is 10 km of mixed paved and rocky terrain.
🩱
Swimsuit + quick-dry towel — pack it for the lighthouse swim stop and any beach time after the hike.
🧢
Moisture-wicking layers, hat, sunglasses — you're exposed on a volcanic ridge with no shade; bring electrolytes, not just water.
🌅
A light dress or outfit to change into for Oia — nice for sunset photos at the Castle after the hike, if you want to freshen up.

💡 Practical Notes
Getting There
Taxis from port to Ephesus ~$15–20 USD each way. ADA Anatolia private tour at ~$90/person all-in is the sweet spot between cost and quality.
Currency & Visas
You're in Turkey — exchange some USD or Euros to Turkish Lira for small vendors. Check current e-Visa requirements for US passport holders before your trip.
Shoes & Sun
Marble streets are uneven and slippery. Closed-toe shoes required. August heat can reach 40°C here. Plan 4L of water per person — this may be the hottest day of the trip.
3D Exit Theatre
At the exit, a 10–15 min 3D animation shows Ephesus in its prime. Worth watching for context after walking the site.
👕 What to Wear

🧢
Wide-brim hat and sunglasses — the site is almost entirely uncovered, with little shade until the exit theatre.

💡 Tips & Logistics
Scooter/ATV Rental
Best way to see beyond town. ~€30–50/day. International license needed. Helmets are mandatory. The lighthouse road is rough — take it slowly.
Crowds & Tender Timing
August is absolute peak season. First tender at 7 AM is crucial. Last tender at 5:45 PM — allow 30+ min queue time in summer. Don't cut it close.
Mykonos Pricing
Greece's most expensive island. Budget €35–50/person for a nice lunch, €15–20 for a cocktail at Little Venice. Worth the splurge — but set expectations accordingly.
The Winds
Mykonos is reliably windy even in summer — bring a light layer for the lighthouse and early morning. The wind makes August here more pleasant than most Greek islands.

💡 Tips & Logistics
Waterfront Walk
The promenade is 3+ km and gorgeous. Flat along the water, hillier toward Ano Poli. Very walkable city with comfortable shoes.
Ano Poli Option
Byzantine walls and towers with panoramic views. 30-min uphill walk from the center — a great add-on for anyone who wants more to explore.
The Food Culture
Thessalonians are obsessed with food. Try bougatsa (custard pastry), koulouri (sesame rings), and Greek coffee from a traditional kafeneio. The food scene here rivals Athens.
Evening Vibe
Thessaloniki comes alive at night. The waterfront, Ladadika district, and Aristotelous Square are beautiful in the evening. You have until 9 PM — use every hour.

Night Before Each Port

Quick Refresher

The single highest-value habit on this list: a 5-minute re-read of each city's story the evening before you arrive. Timing beats volume.

🏛️ Athens
Athena vs. Poseidon's contest for the city, Socrates/Plato/Aristotle, and the Battle of Marathon just outside town.
🌋 Santorini
The Bronze Age eruption that some tie to the Atlantis myth, and the buried Minoan town of Akrotiri, evacuated in time.
⚔️ Rhodes
The Colossus of Rhodes (one of the Seven Wonders) and the Knights Hospitaller's siege-era walls around the Old Town.
🏺 Ephesus
Legend says Amazons founded the city; the Temple of Artemis was a Wonder of the World; Paul's riot at the Great Theatre.
🌀 Mykonos
Said to be where Hercules buried the defeated Giants under the island's boulders — and neighboring Delos, birthplace of Apollo.
🏙️ Thessaloniki
Named for Alexander the Great's half-sister; a major Byzantine city with some of the world's finest surviving mosaics.

Download 3–4 podcast episodes before you leave — don't rely on airport wifi. The night before each port, re-read that city's section above (or the fuller history in each city tab). Since you're in Athens both pre- and post-cruise, one solid read-through up front covers the whole stay — no need to repeat it.

Before You Go

Essential Tips

☀️
August Heat
Greece in August averages 32–38°C. Always start outdoor sightseeing by 8 AM. Carry 2L water per person. Sun hats and light breathable clothing are non-negotiable.
🎫
Book Ahead Now
Book Acropolis tickets online — they sell out in summer. Reserve dinner at Tholoto (Santorini) and Kanoula (Thessaloniki) weeks in advance. Ephesus tickets online too.
👟
Footwear Matters
Every site involves uneven ancient stones and marble. Closed-toe, non-slip shoes for all archaeological sites. Sandals only at the beach. Your feet will thank you by Day 3.
⏰
Tender Ports
Santorini and Mykonos are tender ports — a small boat to shore. Get early tender tickets each morning. Allow 45+ min buffer before the last tender back to ship.
💳
Cash & Cards
Greece is mostly card-friendly. Turkey (Ephesus): bring Lira or USD for small vendors. Always carry €20–50 cash for taxis, tips, and shops that don't take cards.
🚕
Getting Around
Taxis are affordable (€10–30 most rides). Beat app works in Athens. Rhodes and Thessaloniki have good local buses. Scooter/ATV rentals on the islands give great flexibility.
📸
Best Photo Times
Golden hour is magic everywhere. Oia sunset is iconic. Early morning at the Acropolis, Ephesus, and Mykonos windmills gives you soft light and zero crowds.
🍋
Eating Like a Local
Lunch is the main Greek meal (1–3 PM). Dinner starts late — 9 PM is early. Order the "house wine" (χύμα κρασί). Try the freddo espresso — the national cold coffee.
🚢
Airbnb → Piraeus Port
Piraeus is 40 min by Metro (Green Line, direct) or ~€30 by taxi from central Athens. Leave by 2:30 PM for a comfortable 5 PM departure. Don't cut it close in August traffic.
